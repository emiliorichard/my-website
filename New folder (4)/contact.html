Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common legal, procedural, and technical questions regarding the reporting process.

Yes, absolutely. By default, the reporting tool does not require you to provide your name, telephone number, email, or mailing address. Server logs discard IP addresses and user agents for anonymous submissions, and any photo or document attachments are automatically stripped of EXIF GPS coordinates and camera metadata.

Your tip is assigned an encrypted Case Reference ID (e.g. ICE-TIP-2026-89412) and immediately transferred to federal law enforcement intelligence analysts. Analysts review the information against federal law enforcement databases to evaluate veracity, corroborate details, and determine whether to initiate a field investigation with Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) or Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO).

Under Title 18, United States Code, Section 1001, knowingly and willfully providing false, fictitious, or fraudulent statements to federal authorities is a federal felony offense. Penalties include substantial monetary criminal fines and imprisonment of up to five (5) years. Reports intended to harass competitors, neighbors, or individuals based on racial bias are aggressively investigated for prosecution.

If you report anonymously, investigators have no technical ability to contact or subpoena you. If you provide contact details as a confidential whistleblower, your identity is shielded under Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) Exemption 7(D). In the vast majority of cases, tips serve as investigative leads, and federal prosecutors rely on independent physical evidence and warrant-based seizures rather than tipster courtroom testimony.

Never rely on an online form for an active emergency. If anyone is in immediate physical danger, if human trafficking victims are in immediate peril, or if weapons are brandished, immediately call 911 or contact the local police department. You can also call the official ICE Tip Line at 1-866-DHS-2-ICE (1-866-347-2423).
