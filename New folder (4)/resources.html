Understanding Federal Immigration Jurisdictions

Immigration enforcement within the United States is distributed among specialized directorates under the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and Department of Justice (DOJ). Understanding which entity handles specific matters ensures swift, effective response:

Homeland Security Investigations (HSI)

Mission: Criminal investigations arm of ICE. Investigates transnational crimes including commercial human trafficking rings, transnational smuggling cartels, document manufacturing labs, cyber-crimes, and gang operations.

Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO)

Mission: Enforces civil immigration laws. Responsible for identifying, arresting, and managing foreign nationals subject to final orders of removal, fugitive absconders, and noncitizens convicted of felony crimes.

Protections for Victims of Crime (T-Visas & U-Visas)

Federal law recognizes that victims of human trafficking and violent crime must be shielded from coercive deportation. Federal programs provide legal pathways for victims who assist law enforcement:

  • T Nonimmigrant Status (T-Visa): Allows certain victims of human trafficking to remain and work legally in the United States if they cooperate with law enforcement requests for assistance.
  • U Nonimmigrant Status (U-Visa): Protects noncitizen victims of specified qualifying crimes (including domestic violence, involuntary servitude, and felony extortion) who suffer substantial mental or physical abuse.
  • Continued Presence (CP): A temporary immigration status granted to trafficking victims at the request of federal law enforcement agents.
